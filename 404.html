Why use the Hub rather than copy files by hand

Installing a mod in several games means finding folders, checking DLL versions, keeping backups and remembering what needs updating. The Hub does it for you, and shows what it will do before it does it.

By handWith the Hub
Find the game folder, pick a proxy name that is free, copy the right file.
Your Steam, Epic and GOG games are found by themselves; the Hub picks a free name and leaves ReShade, OptiScaler or Special K theirs.
Hunt for the right DLSS and Streamline files, version by version.
The latest DLSS, Ray Reconstruction and frame generation DLLs the mod admits are taken for you, and the Streamline plugins as a matched set. The game's own interposer is never replaced.
Find NVIDIA's Neural Rendering DLL yourself.
nvngx_dlssnr.dll 310.8.0, the exact build the mod requires, is downloaded, verified and put beside the mod.
Redo it all in every game at each new version.
Update all updates the mod, the NVIDIA DLLs and the Streamline plugins in every game. The Hub updates itself too.
Keep your own backups, and hope.
Every replaced file is backed up first; Restore game puts everything back, driver setting included.
Edit a settings file and guess.
A form explains every setting, greys what has no effect on your card, and offers only the presets your game's DLLs take.
Something goes wrong and you do not know why.
Launch and verify watches the game's first minute and says what happened; a diagnostic is ready for a report.

What it does

One game library

The games of Steam, Epic and GOG, the folders you add, or a single game by its executable. Each tile says where the game stands: not installed, installed, to update, changed by the game, failing.

A page for every game

What RTX Encore can do there on your card and driver, frame generation, DLSS render scale, Smooth Motion, Neural Rendering, and why.

The right NVIDIA files

The most recent DLSS and Streamline versions the game's RTX Encore admits, from what your machine already has or from a download. A DLL is accepted only with a valid NVIDIA signature.

Chosen once for every game

Pick the versions of DLSS and Streamline and your DLSS presets once in Settings. What you untick in one game stays left alone there.

Install with a way back

The plan is shown before anything is written, written whole or not at all, and an interrupted install is undone. Administrator rights only where a folder needs them.

Updates

The releases of RTX Encore with their notes, one game or all of them. The Hub looks for its own new version; updates are signed.

Settings

A form for rtx-encore.jsonc, named sets of settings per game, starting settings for your card, and the DLSS Super Resolution model in the driver profile.

Health and backups

Start the game, watch its first minute, and list every state the game can be put back to.

Look before you install

What the Hub says of a game is deduced from its files, your card and your driver. It is not a certification that a feature works in that game, and it measures no performance.

Get started

  1. Download RTX-Encore-Hub-Setup-<version>.exe from the releases and run it. It installs for your account only.
  2. Open Library. Steam, Epic and GOG games are there; add other folders under Settings.
  3. Open a game's page, read what is possible there, and press Install. The plan lists every file before anything is written.
  4. Under Health, Launch and verify starts the game and tells you how its first minute went.
  5. When a new RTX Encore is out, Update all in the library updates every game.
Release candidate. The Hub is published as a pre-release while it gathers feedback. The installer is not code-signed yet: Windows may show a SmartScreen warning the first time, and each release gives the SHA-256 of its installer.
Community profiles: browse, import and share settings per game, ranked for your machine and narrowed by filters. Open since 1.0.0-rc.2; experimental and lightly tested, and it starts empty.
Anti-cheat. Installing a mod in a game an anti-cheat protects can block its startup or put your account at risk. The Hub says so and writes nothing there without your word on the risk.
